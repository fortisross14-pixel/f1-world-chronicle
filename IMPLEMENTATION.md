## Important simulation behavior

### Teams do not know rarity

The player can see `Generational`, `Legend`, `Epic`, `Rare`, `Uncommon` and `Common` because the game is a historical universe viewer. AI teams evaluate an observed-driver model built from current skills, career-year multiplier, form, confidence, results, age, salary, academy information and sponsor value. Future career-curve values are excluded.

### Team finances are organizational

A wealthy main brand is helpful but does not guarantee dominance. The team principal's commercial ability determines how effectively the constructor converts prestige, results and driver-nationality links into secondary deals. Sporting leadership, engineering staff, test-driver feedback, driver salaries and power-unit cost then determine how much money remains for development.

### Constructor changes preserve history

A grid slot has a lineage independent from its current public identity. When a vulnerable constructor leaves, a new main brand may acquire the slot. The archive therefore records that the organization changed name and ownership rather than pretending the old results never existed.

### Weather creates different Saturday and Sunday hierarchies

Qualifying and race conditions are generated independently. Rain can reward wet skill in Q1–Q3 but disappear before the Grand Prix, or arrive during a race after a dry grid was established. The strategy department, tyre crossover, pit timing, driver composure and Safety Car timing then create separate sources of advantage.


### Driver hierarchy is earned, not random

Driver 1 and Driver 2 are recalculated from current effective quality, years with the organization, overall experience, titles, wins, podiums and recent performance. A newly arrived high-potential driver is not automatically the leader because rarity is hidden from teams. A veteran champion can retain Driver 1 status while the prospect develops, but the hierarchy can reverse once the younger driver becomes the stronger established performer.

### Facilities are an economic loop

Aero, simulator, manufacturing, pit crew and academy facilities are rated from 1 to 10. They decay slightly each off-season. Teams with cash or operating surplus invest in their weakest areas, while financially stressed organizations fall behind. Facilities affect setup work, pit-stop execution, reliability, development confidence and the following season's car profile.

### Global time controls

The calendar is shared by all six championships. Simulating one week, four weeks or the rest of the year processes every event whose scheduled week falls inside the selected window. F1 is no longer the clock that indirectly triggers every other series.


### Detail pages mirror the Chronicle structure

Competition Current Year pages now contain one row per race with winner, second, third, winning team, pole and fastest lap, while Standings is a dedicated tab. Driver pages separate Overview, Current Season, Career and Transfers; the Overview renders effective skills and numerical Y1/Y2/Y3 career multipliers. Team pages separate Overview, Personnel, Finance and annual History.

### Young-driver access to F1 is constrained

No active F1 race driver may enter below age 21. An age-21 entrant must come from the elite rarity pool and is evaluated on an early-career multiplier near 0.8, which normally places the driver in a second seat or a developing organization. Market logic applies the same floor every off-season.

### Elite facilities prevent passive cash hoarding

Facility upgrade prices become cubic above level 7 and elite levels decay faster. Teams invest a share of cash above their operating reserve and a share of positive prior balance, prioritizing weak facilities. Technical-director quality and facility quality interact when setting the next car target, so a wealthy team still needs excellent engineers.

## Prototype boundaries

This remains a browser management and history prototype rather than a licensed commercial Formula 1 title. It intentionally does not include:

- Official logos, driver likenesses, copyrighted photography or proprietary circuit maps
- Real-time 3D racing
- A server account system or automatic cloud synchronization
- A full legal and licensing layer for publishing a commercial product with real brands
- A visual car painter; liveries are generated from main-brand and sponsor colors in reusable SVG form

The current race presentation is data-first: classifications, session context, weather, strategy, incidents, standings and generated narratives. The data model can support a later live-timing or animated race layer without replacing the universe systems.
